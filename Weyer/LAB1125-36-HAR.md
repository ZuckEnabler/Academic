### Laboratory Conservation Log

**Project:** AAV, Fondo Miscellaneo, Serie Latina, Pos. 47, fasc. 3
**Object:** ff. 120r–120v, severely damaged bifolium
**Date:** 18 November 2025
**Examiner:** E. A. Harcourt

**09:10 — Initial examination**

The two leaves selected for examination are by far the most degraded of the surviving material. Both exhibit extensive abrasion, foxing, localized darkening, and numerous losses along the outer margins. Ink remains visible in fragments, but in several areas the writing has apparently been reduced to little more than discoloration within the fibres of the paper. The lower half of f. 120v is particularly troublesome; under ordinary illumination it appears almost entirely illegible.

No attempt was made to flatten, humidify, or mechanically clean the leaves at this stage. High-resolution photography was completed under diffuse, raking, and transmitted illumination. A preliminary ultraviolet examination produced several faint differences in fluorescence, although these were insufficient to permit a confident reading.

**10:35 — Surface examination and imaging**

Raking illumination proved considerably more useful than anticipated. Several strokes of apparently lost writing became visible as shallow interruptions in the surface of the paper. These do not correspond neatly with the surviving ink on the recto, suggesting either abrasion after writing or the presence of writing which has subsequently become almost completely fugitive.

Multispectral images were taken at several wavelengths. The results indicate that portions of the obscured text may still possess a chemical contrast not apparent to the naked eye. A number of short vertical and diagonal strokes are clearly distinguishable beneath the discoloured areas.

**13:20 — Consolidation test**

A minute detached fragment from an already missing portion was used for preliminary adhesive testing. A dilute solution of **Paraloid B-72** was applied to the fragment and allowed to dry under controlled conditions. The treatment produced acceptable consolidation without immediately obscuring the remaining surface characteristics.

Given the historical importance and extreme fragility of the leaves, treatment was therefore restricted to localized consolidation of friable fibres. **No broad application of Paraloid was undertaken.** The principal purpose of the treatment was stabilization rather than visual alteration or reconstruction.

**15:05 — Optical recovery**

Following stabilization, the leaves were examined again under oblique illumination and magnification. The improvement is unexpectedly substantial.

On f. 120r, several characters previously thought to be losses can now be distinguished. More remarkable is f. 120v, where faint traces beneath the principal abrasion appear to form continuous words rather than isolated marks. The writing is extremely faint and cannot yet be transcribed with confidence.

A photographic comparison with the untreated images confirms that these are not simply artefacts introduced by the conservation process.

**17:40 — Preliminary transcription**

The recovered material appears to contain another description of the same creature mentioned elsewhere in the letter. The surviving phrases are fragmentary, but one passage seems to describe the creature standing upright and regarding the writer directly. A second line may contain a reference to its teeth, although this reading remains uncertain.

Further multispectral examination is required before any portion of the restored text is incorporated into the working transcription.

**Conclusion:** The treatment has stabilized the most severely damaged material and, in conjunction with multispectral and raking-light imaging, has revealed previously invisible features of the writing. The leaves should now be regarded as **partially recoverable rather than irretrievably lost**. No reconstruction should yet be considered definitive.
