15 November 2025

Dear Prof. Moretti,

I have at last had an opportunity to examine the fragment I mentioned from the restricted holdings of the Vatican Apostolic Archives. Its provisional reference is AAV, Fondo Miscellaneo, Serie Latina, Pos. 47, fasc. 3, Scaffale IV, fila 2, ff. 118r–121v. The foliation appears to be later than the original manuscript, and at least two leaves seem to have been misplaced, so I would hesitate to regard the sequence as definitive.

The document is a late-sixteenth-century letter attributed, with some reservations, to Johann Wier. Much of the text has suffered from abrasion, staining, and tearing, and several passages can presently be restored only conjecturally. Raking light has recovered portions of otherwise illegible lines, though considerable lacunae remain. Most curious are the repeated accounts of a strange anthropomorphic deer, described with remarkable consistency in several encounters, some apparently occurring in waking life and others in dreams. The creature is never given a proper name in the surviving text.

I am increasingly struck by the possibility that this may constitute a very early account of what is now referred to, in certain contemporary circles, as the Marshmallow Forest. I emphasize that this is only a hypothesis. The surviving passages contain no reference to that name, nor do they establish any connection with the modern phenomenon. The suggestion rests principally upon the peculiar nature of the creature described and the author's repeated uncertainty as to whether he is dealing with a natural being, a spirit, or something produced by the imagination. Even so, I think the possibility merits further consideration.

I have attached my provisional transcription of ff. 119r–120v. The most damaged portions are marked accordingly, and I have resisted the temptation to fill the gaps where the evidence does not warrant it.

Yours sincerely,
Edward A. Harcourt
Department of History
University of Cambridge
